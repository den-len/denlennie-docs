Your morning briefing.

Connect your Gmail, Calendar and Slack to Claude once, and from tomorrow you just open it and ask for your day: a briefing and a task list pulled straight from your own calendar and inbox. A one-minute setup, then a five-second habit. Not a build, a login.

01 · Connect once

Three connectors, about a minute

  1. In Claude, open Cowork and click Customize in the left sidebar, then Connectors. The screen splits into web connectors and desktop connectors. The ones you want, Gmail, Google Calendar and Slack, are web connectors.
  2. Click the + icon, then Browse Connectors. Find Gmail near the top, click the + next to it. A Google sign-in window opens: pick your account, review the permissions, click Continue. Gmail now shows as connected. Under a minute.
  3. Do the same for Google Calendar, then Slack. Same three moves each time: plus icon, pick the connector, authorise. Once you've done Gmail, you've got the whole pattern.

Good to know

  • It only sees what you see. Claude mirrors your own login and permissions. It can't reach anything you can't reach yourself.
  • Nothing changes without you. Write and delete actions need your approval. Calendar edits, sending email, none of it happens silently. Claude shows you what it's about to do first, so this can read your day without touching it.
One thing to know. The Google connector links one Google account at a time, and Gmail, Calendar and Drive all ride on it. If your business and personal calendars sit on different logins, connect the one that holds your working day. Multiple accounts aren't supported yet, the simple workaround is forwarding from the other account into the connected one.

02 · Ask for your day

The prompt

Once the three are connected, paste this in, or just say it. The first time is a setup; after that it's a five-second habit.

The prompt 
Give me my briefing for the day ahead.

From my calendar today: a quick summary first (how many meetings, my first and last, any big gaps), then each meeting in order with the time, who's on it, and a one-line note on what it's about or what I should prepare.

From my email: anything that needs a reply today, and anything tied to one of today's meetings.

Then a short, prioritised task list: the three or four things that actually matter today, in order.

Keep it tight and scannable. Don't change anything, just tell me what's on.

After the first run, "What's on today?" is enough. You taught it the shape once, now it just answers.
