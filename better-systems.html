"I need better systems in my business": where to actually start

When an owner says they need better systems, they rarely mean software. They mean everything lives in their head, and they're the person who has to see everything. The fix isn't a new tool or the perfect system. Write down how things are done now. Record yourself doing a task, then turn that recording into steps.

Why it happens

I hear "I just need better systems" on almost every sales call. Then I ask a simple question: if you disappeared tomorrow, would the business fall over? The answer is usually yes. That's the real problem the word "systems" is covering.

It happens because video businesses grow faster than their structure. The owner goes from freelancer to employer, skips the systems step, and ends up as the bottleneck. The team can deliver a project, but nobody can run a process without asking.

The usual reaction is to buy software. Software doesn't fix it. Keep two things separate: project management is who does what by when, and documentation is where the how lives. Owners who try to store their systems inside their project management tool end up in a mess.

Here's where to start, as a certified Systemologist and someone who was a case study in the Systemology book:

  1. Map the critical client flow. How work moves through the business, from the enquiry coming in to the invoice being paid. Keep it simple enough that someone who has never seen your business could follow it.
  2. Document what doesn't change. How you invoice won't change. How you run a pre-production brief won't change much. Start there.
  3. Skip how to film. That's the part your people already know.
  4. Record, then write. The video is the rough-and-ready training. The written steps come from the video.

None of this is glamorous. Business done right is boring: predictable, consistent and profitable. That's the point.

Try this

Document what exists, not what's ideal. A rough screen recording plus a short list of steps beats a perfect manual that never gets written. Over about twelve weeks, the business gets out of your head and you stop being the bottleneck.

Hold yourself accountable

Which of these have you taken on or put in place recently?

  • Your critical client flow, from enquiry to paid invoice, is written down on one page.
  • At least one process that never changes, such as invoicing, is documented.
  • Your documentation lives somewhere separate from your project management tool.
  • Someone on your team has followed one of your documented processes without asking you.

Your one move this week

What's the one thing you can commit to implementing this week? If you're not sure, start here.

Pick the process that never changes, probably invoicing. Record yourself doing it once, then write the steps underneath the recording. That's your first system.
