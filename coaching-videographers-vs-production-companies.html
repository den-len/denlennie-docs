Why do videographers and production company owners need different coaching?

Videographers and production company owners need different coaching because the thing holding each one back is different, and coaching aimed at the wrong constraint adds work without moving the business.

When you are the business, effort is the growth strategy, and effort has a ceiling. There are only so many days you can sell before fatigue, mistakes or burnout show up. More clients, more gear and more late nights add pressure rather than momentum. What moves things at this point is focus: one offer, one type of client, one way work comes in.

Once there's a team, the constraint moves. Work comes in, money flows, and everything still runs through you for approval. Your team waits for you because nobody has defined what they own. You can take a week off but not a month. That's the operator ceiling: revenue stops at what one person can personally hold together, and the fix is structural.

Many owners place themselves one step further along than they are. The reliable test is what's actually in place in the business, which is what the self-check below asks about.

How to work out which coaching you need: the method, step by step

The method is a ten-line self-check against what's true in your business this month, then one question to put to any coach you're considering.

  1. Fill in the self-check. Answer each line yes or no, based on what's in place today, not what's planned.

Copy and paste: the ten-line self-check

Foundations

1. My niche is one specific market, written down. [yes or no]

2. I have one packaged offer with a price floor. [yes or no]

3. I have a minimum project fee, and I hold it with no exceptions. [yes or no]

4. One sales activity runs every week, whether I'm busy or not. [yes or no]

5. I know which clients and projects actually make money. [yes or no]

Structure

6. My pricing is written down: day rates, project minimums, package options. [yes or no]

7. My delivery process is written down, from brief to final delivery. [yes or no]

8. At least one freelancer or team member has run a job without me overseeing it. [yes or no]

9. I'm not shooting or editing on any project. [yes or no]

10. The business could run for a month without my daily involvement. [yes or no]

  1. Find your first no. If any of lines 1 to 5 is a no, your coaching need is foundations, whatever your revenue. If lines 1 to 5 are all yes and lines 6 to 10 are mostly no, your need is structure.
  2. Write down the question that keeps coming back. If it's "where does the next client come from", that usually confirms foundations. If it's "why am I still on every shoot when we're this busy", that usually confirms structure. If the question and the self-check disagree, trust the self-check.
  3. Ask the coach who they're built for. Most coaches serve one of the two needs better than the other. Ask before you join.

Copy and paste: the question to ask any coach

Most of the owners you work with: are they solo, or running a team? What does a typical member's business look like when they join, and what's the first thing you work on with them?

  1. Match the answer to your first no. A coach whose typical member looks like you, and whose first piece of work matches your first no, is worth a closer look.

The mistakes that send owners to the wrong coaching

Four mistakes send owners to coaching built for a different business.

  • Buying for the business you want. The instinct to work on hiring or scale is often right and early. Close the first no before moving on.
  • Using revenue as the test. Revenue says you can pay. It doesn't say what's in place.
  • Systemising before the offer is clear. Simplify first, systemise second, and never build complex systems on top of chaos.
  • Hiring before the process is on paper. Hiring and automation make sense once you've written down what gets handed over. Until then, the person you hire has nothing to work from except you.

The rule of thumb for coaching by stage

Coach the constraint you have today: foundations until lines 1 to 5 are all yes, structure after that. Benchmark: the ten-line self-check, filled in against what's in place this month, with your first no as the starting point for any coaching conversation.
