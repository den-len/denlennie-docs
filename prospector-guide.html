What it is

The Prospector Engine is a tool built for video production company owners who want to find new clients without spending hours on manual research.

You tell it about your business: who you serve, what you offer, the kind of companies you want to work with. It searches the web, finds companies that match, identifies the right decision-makers at each one, and delivers a ranked list of prospects with contact details and a personalised outreach angle for every lead.

Instead of scrolling LinkedIn, googling directories, and copy-pasting contacts into a spreadsheet, the engine does that for you in minutes. You get a ready-to-use CSV with scored, prioritised leads and a clear reason to reach out to each one.

Before you start. The more specific you are about who you serve, the better your results. The engine is only as sharp as the profile you give it. Five minutes spent on the profile saves you a list full of near-misses.

What you put in

The engine needs to understand your business before it can find the right prospects. Here is what you provide.

  • Your website URL. Paste it and the engine scans your site automatically: homepage, about, services, and other key pages. From that it drafts a profile of what you do, who you serve, and the industries you work in. About 30 seconds, no forms to fill by hand if the scan reads your site correctly.
  • A short business description (optional, recommended). If your site is outdated or your positioning has shifted, add a sentence. Something like "Video production company specialising in corporate training content for financial services firms in the UK." It takes priority over the website and steers the AI away from guesswork.
  • Your client roster (optional). Upload a CSV of past or current clients and the engine learns the pattern: industries, size, what they have in common, and uses it to find lookalikes. Company names and websites are enough. Industry, location or notes columns make it stronger. This is the single most powerful thing you can do to improve your results.

Your Ideal Client Profile

After the scan, you review and refine your profile. For each field the AI suggests options based on what it found, and you pick the best fit or write your own. Every field is editable. The AI gives you a starting point. You keep control.

  • Business description: what your company does, in a sentence or two.
  • What you offer: your specific services, deliverables, and what sets you apart.
  • Target market: the type of organisation you want to reach, not individual people.
  • Geography: where your ideal clients are based. Be as specific as you can.
  • Target industries: the sectors you want to work in. Check or uncheck each one.
  • Ideal client signals: what makes a prospect a great fit, such as "runs annual events" or "has a marketing team."
  • Red flags: what makes a prospect a poor fit, such as "volunteer-run with no budget."
  • Decision-maker titles: the job titles of the people who would buy from you, such as CEO or Head of Marketing.
  • Budget signals: clues that a company can afford your services.

The four-step process

1
Tab · Your Business Profile

Profile your business

Paste your website URL and click Scan Website. The AI reads your site and drafts your Ideal Client Profile. Walk through each section and refine it: pick from the suggestions, edit them, or write your own. Upload your client roster here too if you have one. When you are happy, click Confirm Profile & Continue, then Save Client Profile. This profile drives everything that follows.

2
Tab · Find Prospects

Find prospects

The engine generates a discovery brief from your profile: target market, geography, industries, signals to look for, red flags to avoid. It is ready to go as-is, and you can edit it if you want. Click Find Prospects and the AI searches the web, working through industry categories and building a list. A live progress bar shows how many it has found. Three to eight minutes, depending on how many you ask for. The default is 50. When it finishes you get a summary: total prospects, how many have websites, emails, and LinkedIn pages.

3
Tab · Enrich & Score

Enrich and score

This turns a raw list into an outreach-ready database. Click Run Pipeline and the engine works through three stages: it visits each website and pulls signals (team size, events, sponsors, video investment); it finds the decision-makers and their contact details; then it scores every prospect 0 to 10, assigns a priority tier, a confidence level, and writes a personalised outreach angle. Five to fifteen minutes, depending on list size.

4
Tab · Your Leads

Export your leads

Your scored, ranked results appear in a table you can filter by tier, state, and confidence. Click into any organisation for the full detail: score, contacts, outreach angle, and the signals behind the score. When you are ready, click Download Full CSV for everything in a spreadsheet.

What you get back

A complete dossier for every prospect

Your CSV is outreach-ready: organisation, location and website; decision-maker names, titles and LinkedIn profiles; verified emails and phone numbers where available; an ICP score and priority tier; a confidence level; a one-line positioning summary; and the outreach angle, a two to three sentence reason to make contact that references real evidence from their site. There are blank status and notes columns so you can track your outreach as you work.

The priority tiers

Sort by tier and you know where to spend your time first.

A
A-tier, score 8 to 10. Exceptional fit. Your highest-priority prospects. Start here.
B
B-tier, score 6 to 7. Strong fit. Well worth pursuing.
C
C-tier, score 4 to 5. Moderate fit. Worth a look, lower priority.
D
D-tier, score 0 to 3. Poor fit. Probably not worth your time right now.

How to use your results

Start at the top

Sort by priority tier and start with your A-tier prospects. They have the right signals, the right budget indicators, and a clear reason to work with you.

Use the outreach angle

Every prospect has a custom reason to reach out, built from real evidence on their site. Do not send a generic message. Use the angle as the foundation for your connection request, email or call.

Mark good fit and poor fit

Click any organisation and leave feedback: good fit, poor fit, contacted, meeting booked, or won. Add a note. The engine reads it on your next run, avoiding poor-fit patterns and favouring good ones. Your results get better every time.

Refine and run again

Your first run is a calibration. Look at the A-tier results. Do they feel right? If not, adjust your signals, industries or geography and run again. Each iteration gets sharper.

Tips for the best results

  • Be specific about geography. "United Kingdom" is fine, but "London and the South East" or "Manchester and the North West" gives more targeted results. The engine searches differently when it knows exactly where to look.
  • Add a business description when you scan. If your site is ambiguous, perhaps you do both weddings and corporate but only want corporate, a single sentence steers the AI. It takes priority over the website.
  • Upload your client roster. Real examples beat a profile description. Even company names and websites give the AI something to learn from.
  • Uncheck irrelevant industries before confirming. The AI suggests adjacent industries. Some land, some do not. Unchecking the wrong ones stops the engine wasting lookups on prospects you would never pursue.
  • Make your signals precise. Instead of "has a marketing team," try "has a dedicated marketing or communications team with at least one full-time hire." The sharper the signal, the more precisely each prospect is scored.
  • Run once, review, then refine. Treat the first run as calibration. The second run is noticeably better.
