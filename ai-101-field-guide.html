Chat, Cowork, Code.

The companion to the AI 101 session. The three ways into Claude and when to use each, the words you'll hear, and a first look at a Claude Code folder. Keep it nearby. When a term lands that you don't recognise, look it up here instead of nodding along.

The three doors

Chat, Cowork and Code all run on the same Claude models. What changes is how much Claude knows about you when you open it, and what it can touch while it works. Think of it as a ladder of context. Each step up, it knows more before you type a word.

Door one

Chat

Isolated incidents. You ask, it answers, you close it. Each new conversation starts close to blank. Projects and memory carry a little across, but the real brief still lives in your head.

Knows when you open it
Close to nothing.
Best for
One-off thinking and writing.
The catch
You retype who you are and how you work, every time.

Door two

Cowork

Some context. Inside the Claude desktop app, you hand it a folder on your computer and the apps you've connected, give it a job, and it works through the steps and hands back finished files.

Knows when you open it
What's in the folder you give it.
Best for
A task with files to read and files to hand back.
The catch
No running file on how you think, unless you write one.

Door three

Code

A folder that learns how you think. You won't write code. Claude Code runs in the same desktop app, pointed at one folder you own, and reads it before it touches anything. Corrections go into the file, so they stick.

Knows when you open it
Your whole folder, every time.
Best for
The jobs you do every week, the same way.
The catch
An afternoon to set up, then a habit to keep.
How often you do the jobUseExamples from a video business
Once.ChatRewrite a quote email so it doesn't sound apologetic. Three ways to price a two-day shoot. A script idea for a case study.
Now and then, with files.CoworkA call sheet built from three client briefs. A tidy-up of a shoot folder. Every testimonial from this year's inbox pulled into one document.
Every week, the same way.CodeChasing quotes that have gone quiet. Discovery call notes into a proposal. Replies to enquiries in your voice, waiting in your drafts.

The test: if you've explained the same thing to Chat three times, it belongs in a file. Save it as a Project in Chat, or a folder in Cowork. That's your first context file, and it's the same habit Code is built on.

Which model for which job

Every AI company sells roughly the same range: a big, slower, more thoughtful model, a middle everyday model, and a small fast one. Model names change every few months. The three tiers stay. Think of it like camera bodies: you don't bring the cinema rig to shoot a quick behind-the-scenes clip, and you don't shoot the hero brand film on your phone.

The jobReach forWhy
Deep thinking. Strategy, pricing decisions, reviewing a contract, planning a quarter. The biggest model on offer (Claude Opus, or the top tier from OpenAI or Google). Slower, but a pricing decision is worth thirty seconds of waiting.
Everyday writing. Emails, proposals, social posts, call summaries. The middle tier (Claude Sonnet or equivalent). Fast and capable enough to use fifty times a day. Most of your week lives here.
High volume, simple jobs. Tagging leads, sorting lists, pulling names out of transcripts. The small fast tier (Claude Haiku or equivalent). When the task is simple and you're running it hundreds of times, speed and cost win.
Research that needs sources. Checking a market, background on a prospect. Any model with web search or a research mode turned on. Models answer from training data unless they can search. For anything current, make it look things up.

Which tool for which task

You'll hear all of these mentioned. You don't need all of them. Most owners settle on one or two and use them well.

ChatGPT (OpenAI)
The capable all-rounder that started the wave. Quick to answer, good at drafting and brainstorming. Like a keen intern: fast, but it needs a clear brief and a check on its work. Best for: everyday drafting, quick answers, thinking out loud.
Gemini (Google)
Google's model, and the one to reach for when the job touches Google's world: reading websites, Search and YouTube, broad marketing research. Best for: web and YouTube research, broad marketing scans.
Claude (Anthropic)
Used here for careful reasoning and writing in your voice. Three doors: Chat, Cowork and Code, covered above. Best for: writing that sounds like you, working with your documents, running the jobs you repeat.
NotebookLM (Google)
Feed it a pile of your own material (transcripts, reports, long PDFs) and it answers questions grounded in what you gave it, rather than the open web. Best for: digesting large documents and transcripts you provide.

The AI words

Model (or LLM)
The AI itself. LLM stands for large language model: a system trained on enormous amounts of text that predicts what should come next. Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini are products built around models. Why you care: "which model" is just "which engine". Pick by the job, not the brand ad.
Prompt
Whatever you type to the model. A question, an instruction, a half-formed thought. Why you care: vague prompt, vague answer. The fix is rarely a better model. It's a clearer brief, the same as briefing a freelancer.
Context
Everything the model knows about you and the job at the moment it starts working: what you typed, the files it can see, the instructions it read first. Why you care: this is the whole game. The three doors are three different amounts of it.
System prompt (or custom instructions)
Standing instructions the model reads before every conversation. Your voice rules, your spelling, your banned words. Why you care: it's the difference between sounding like you and sounding like every other AI-written post.
Project
In Claude Chat, a workspace that keeps your instructions and uploaded files together, so every conversation inside it starts with them. Why you care: the easiest first step up from blank-page chat. One Project per repeat job.
Context window
The model's working memory: how much it can hold in mind at once. Big, but not endless. When a chat gets very long, the earliest parts fall out of view. Why you care: long rambling chats drift. Fresh conversation for a fresh job, and keep the permanent material in files.
Token
The unit models read and bill in. Roughly three quarters of a word. Why you care: mostly if you pay per use.
Hallucination
When a model states something false with complete confidence: an invented statistic, a made-up quote, a client name that doesn't exist. Why you care: never send AI-drafted numbers, names or claims to a client without checking them. It isn't lying, it's pattern-matching, but the damage is the same.
Agent
An AI that doesn't just answer, it acts: reads files, runs steps, checks its own work, carries a task through to a finish. Cowork and Code both work this way. Why you care: chat is you doing the work with help. An agent is the work getting done while you do something else.
Connector (MCP)
A plug that lets Claude reach your other tools: email, calendar, Google Drive, your CRM. MCP (Model Context Protocol) is the standard underneath. One connector per tool. Why you care: it's how AI stops being a writing tool and starts touching your actual inbox and pipeline. Start with one.
API and API key
An API is how two pieces of software talk to each other without anyone clicking buttons. An API key is the password that lets a tool use one on your account, and bill your card for it. Why you care: treat keys like bank details. Never in a shared doc, a chat, or a folder that syncs anywhere.
Training cutoff
The date the model's built-in knowledge stops. Anything later, it only knows if it searches the web or you tell it. Why you care: asking about last month without search turned on is asking it to guess.

The Claude Code words

Only needed if you open door three. You'll see these on screen in the live part of the session.

CLAUDE.md
A plain text file at the top of your folder that Claude Code reads first, every session: who you are, what you sell, how you sound, how you want it to work. Why you care: it's the brief you'd otherwise retype into Chat every day. Correct Claude once and the correction goes here.
Markdown (.md)
Plain text with a few symbols for formatting: # for a heading, ** for bold, - for a bullet. Readable by any computer, any app, any AI. Why you care: no proprietary format, no app that can shut down and take your files with it.
Local vs cloud
Local means on your computer's own disk. Cloud means on someone else's server: GitHub, your CRM, the model itself. Why you care: the working folder lives locally, not inside Dropbox, iCloud or Google Drive, because sync tools corrupt live working folders. The backup lives in the cloud.
Git and repository (repo)
Git tracks every change to a folder so you can wind back to any earlier version. A repo is a folder git is tracking. Why you care: version history for your business brain. Break something on Tuesday, restore Monday.
GitHub
A website that holds a private copy of your repo in the cloud. Why you care: the off-site backup of what becomes one of the most valuable folders you own. Same discipline as backing up rushes.
Commit, push and pull
A commit is a saved snapshot with a note ("updated day rates"). Push sends your snapshots up to GitHub. Pull brings the latest copy down before you start. Why you care: commit when something's worth keeping, push at the end of the day, pull before you start. Claude does all three when you ask.
Plan mode
A setting where Claude Code talks the job through and shows you its plan before it changes anything. Why you care: thirty seconds of conversation up front gets a much better first version. Use it for anything new.

A first look at a Claude Code folder

This is the teaser from the session, not a build guide. If you want to try it, set aside an afternoon, use the eleven-minute starter video in the group, and keep it to these four moves.

  • A folder on your own drive. Make one folder on your computer's disk, outside Dropbox, iCloud and Google Drive. Open the Claude desktop app, go to Code, and point it at that folder. No terminal needed.
  • Back it up to GitHub. A free account and a private repository. Ask Claude to set up the backup and walk you through it. From then on, "commit and push" at the end of a session is the habit.
  • Teach it how you think. Start a CLAUDE.md with half a page. The template below is enough. It grows every time you correct something.
  • Connect your email. So Claude can read a thread and leave a reply in your drafts folder. It drafts. You read it. You press send. Keep it that way.

A starter CLAUDE.md. Copy it, fill in the brackets, save it in your folder as CLAUDE.md.

# About my business
I run [company name], a video production company in [city].
We make [types of video] for [types of client].
A typical project is [range]. My day rate is [figure].

# How I sound
[Short sentences. British spelling. No exclamation marks.]
Words I never use: [e.g. "just checking in", "touching base"].

# How you work with me
- Draft, never send. I read everything before it goes.
- Ask before you change anything outside this folder.
- Check names, numbers and dates against my files. If you can't check it, say so.
- When I correct you, add the correction to this file.

# The one job you do for me first
[e.g. Find quotes I sent that have had no reply for 5 working days,
and draft a short follow-up for each in my drafts folder.]

Rabbit holes to skip, for now

  • Lots of agents. You need one that does a job you're sick of. Use it for two weeks before you build the next.
  • Every connector on day one. Start with email. Add the next when a job asks for it.
  • Anything that sends on its own. Drafts only. Automatic sending is how one bad day reaches forty clients.
  • Building an app. Tempting, and a month gone. The folder and the file come first.
  • Swapping tools every week. Two tools used well beat ten used badly.

Four rules, whichever door you use

  • It drafts. You send. Nothing reaches a client until you've read it.
  • Check names, numbers and dates. Every time.
  • No passwords or keys in the folder or the chat. They're bank details.
  • Know where client data goes. Check the account's data settings before a client's contract or financials go in.

Your next two weeks

  1. Pick one job you're sick of doing. Quote follow-ups, call notes into a brief, the reply you keep putting off.
  2. Run it in Chat with a proper brief. Who you are, who it's for, how you sound, what good looks like.
  3. The third time you retype that brief, save it. A Project in Chat, or a folder in Cowork.
  4. Curious about Code? The starter video in the group, one folder, one CLAUDE.md, the same one job.

Post what you made in the group, or bring it to the next call.

The operating principle

Less AI. Used better.

Every term in this guide describes machinery, and machinery isn't the point. What counts is sharp context: who they serve, how they sound, what they charge, and why. The files carry that. The tools just read them.
