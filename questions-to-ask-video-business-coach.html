Questions to ask a video business coach before you hire them

Ask about seven things before you sign up: whether the coach has run a video production business, what their results are and how they're measured, the full terms, what happens in the first month, how the coaching runs between sessions, who else is in the room, and who it isn't for. Then listen for the questions they ask you, and check one claim yourself before you pay.

What should you ask a video business coach before you sign up?

Ask a video business coach the questions below in writing, so you can lay the answers side by side and see who answers plainly.

Copy and paste: the questions to send a coach before you sign up

Experience

1. Have you run a video production business yourself? For how long, and at what size?

2. Which kinds of production company do you work with most, and at what revenue?

Proof

3. What's your average client result, and how do you measure it?

4. How long does the average client stay?

5. Where can I read or watch a client's account in their own words, with the business identified?

Terms

6. What's the monthly price, the minimum term, and anything paid up front?

7. How do I cancel, and what's your refund policy?

The first month

8. What happens in my first month, and what will you need from me?

9. How long before I should expect to see a change in the numbers?

Between sessions

10. How does the coaching run between sessions, and how do I reach you when I'm at a decision point?

11. How much time should I budget each week, including the work between sessions?

The room

12. Who else is in the group, and what standard do they work to? What happens when someone doesn't keep it?

Fit

13. Who isn't this for?

14. How will we both know, early on, if it isn't working?

What do good answers from a coach sound like?

Good answers from a coach are plain and specific, with numbers where numbers belong and the terms in writing. A vague or deferred answer is information too, and worth a follow-up question.

  • Have you run a video production business? Clear: years, size and what the company made. Follow up if the answer moves straight to credentials or "business is business".
  • Average client result. Clear: a figure, a period, and how it was measured. Follow up if there are stories but no average.
  • How long clients stay. Clear: an average in months. Follow up if there's no figure.
  • The full terms. Clear: price, minimum term, up-front fees and cancellation, in writing. Follow up if the answer is "we can go through that on a call".
  • The first month. Clear: a diagnosis on your own numbers before any advice. Follow up if there's a fixed plan before they've seen your numbers.
  • How long before results. Clear: months, with a reason. Follow up if the promise is a fast fix.
  • Who isn't this for. Clear: a specific description. Follow up if the answer is "it works for everyone".

Notice whether they ask about your business before they prescribe for it. The first conversation should feel like being understood, not being closed.

What questions should a good coach ask you?

A good coach asks questions that test whether you're a fit: what standing still costs you, what you changed because of earlier advice, who you check decisions with, whether you're reachable when you're in delivery, and how you handle the uncomfortable part of the work.

  • What does standing still cost you, and by when does it have to change? A specific cost with a date attached predicts follow-through better than anything else. "Growth would be nice" doesn't.
  • What did you change because of coaching or advice you've had before? Someone with a long coaching history who can't point to one thing they implemented is usually shopping for agreement.
  • Who do you check decisions with? It shows where your advice really comes from, and whether it's calibrated to the clients you want.
  • When you're in delivery, are you reachable? Not a disqualifier, but if a busy season takes you offline for weeks, it should be raised before you join.
  • What happens when the next step means sending a price or making the call? The first ask that requires exposure is where most stalls happen, so a coach will want to know how you handle it.

Notice what a coach doesn't lean on as well. Your revenue tells them you can pay, which is the smallest question. A coach who checks fit is protecting the room you'd be joining.

How do you check a coach's claims before you sign?

Check a coach's claims by picking the most checkable thing they've told you and verifying it yourself before you pay.

That might be a client result you can find in the client's own words, a book you can read, free material that shows how they think, or terms published on a page you can read again later. If the first claim you check holds up, the rest of the conversation is easier to trust. If it doesn't, you've learnt that cheaply.
