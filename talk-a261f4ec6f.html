The rest of the vocabulary.

Big, but not endless. Long chats drift because the start falls out of view. New job, new chat.

Confidently wrong.

05The three doors

Same model, different memory.

Chat, Cowork and Code all run on Claude. The difference is what it's carrying when the conversation starts, and what it can touch while it works.

06The context ladder

Each step up, it knows more before you type.

You don't have to climb all three. Most of you will live on the first two for a while, and that's fine.

07Door one

Chat: isolated incidents.

You open a conversation, ask, get an answer, close it. Next time, it starts close to blank. Projects and memory carry a little across, but the real brief still lives in your head, and you retype it every time.

Good for one-off thinking. Tiring for anything you do every week.

Good forRewrite this quote email so it doesn't sound apologetic.
Good forThree ways to price a two-day shoot with a half-day edit.
The tax"I run a video company in Leeds, we do corporate, our day rate is [figure]" Again.
08Door two

Cowork: some context.

Inside the Claude desktop app. You hand it a folder on your computer and the apps you've connected, give it a job, and it works through the steps and hands back finished files. It knows what's in that folder today. It doesn't keep a running file on how you think unless you write one.

The step from asking questions to handing over a task.

Good forRead the three client briefs in this folder and draft a call sheet for Thursday.
Good forSort these 200 receipts into a spreadsheet by month and supplier.
Good forPull every testimonial from my inbox this year into one document.
09Door three

Code: a folder that learns how you think.

Don't let the name put you off. You won't write code. Claude Code runs in the same desktop app, pointed at one folder you own. That folder holds your prices, your clients, your voice and your rules, and it reads all of it before it touches anything. When you correct it, the correction goes in the file. Next week it doesn't make that mistake.

The difference is that it keeps what you teach it.

11Which door for which job

Match the door to how often you do it.

  1. Once. Chat. A tricky client email, a pricing sanity check, a script idea.
  2. Now and then, with files. Cowork. A call sheet from three briefs, a tidy-up of a shoot folder, a summary of a pile of documents.
  3. Every week, the same way. Code. Quote follow-ups, discovery call notes into a proposal, replies in your voice.

If you've explained the same thing to Chat three times, it belongs in a file.

13The four moves

What setting one up looks like.

01
A folder on your own drive.
On your computer, or in Dropbox or Google Drive. Not iCloud: it swaps files for empty placeholders to save space.
02
Back it up to GitHub.
A private copy in the cloud, with every version kept. Lose the laptop, lose nothing. Claude does the saving when you ask.
03
Teach it how you think.
A CLAUDE.md file: your business, your prices, how you sound, how you want it to work. It reads this first, every time.
04
Connect your email.
So it can read the thread and leave a reply in your drafts. It drafts. You read it. You press send.
14Move three, up close

The first page it reads.

Plain text. No special software. This is what turns a clever stranger into someone who's worked with you for a year. Start with half a page. It grows.

CLAUDE.md
My business
Corporate and brand video for [type of client] in [region]. Typical project [range]. Day rate [figure].
How I sound
Short sentences. British spelling. No exclamation marks. Never "just checking in".
How you work
Draft, never send. Ask before touching anything outside this folder. When I correct you, add it here.
Your first job
Chase quotes quiet for 5 working days

A starter version is in the guide.

15Why it gets better

Why it improves with use.

It drafts You correct It writes the fix into the file Next draft starts smarter

In Chat, most corrections stay in that conversation. In a folder, they're written down and read every time. A few months of small corrections leaves a file that reflects how you work, built from your clients and your judgement.

17Rabbit holes

Skip these. For now.

  1. Lots of agents. You need one that does a job you're sick of. Use it for two weeks before you build the next.
  2. Every connector on day one. Start with email. Add the next one when a job asks for it.
  3. Anything that sends on its own. Drafts only. Automatic sending is how one bad day reaches forty clients.
  4. Building an app. Tempting, and a month gone. The folder and the file come first.
  5. Swapping tools every week. Two tools used well beat ten used badly.
18Guardrails

Four rules, whichever door you use.

It drafts. You send.
Nothing reaches a client until you've read it.
Check names, numbers, dates.
Every time. It pattern-matches, and a confident wrong figure costs the same as a lie.
No passwords in the folder.
Not in the files, not in a chat. API keys are bank details.
Know where client data goes.
Check the account's data settings before a client's contract or numbers go in.
19The next two weeks

One job for the next two weeks.

  1. Pick one job you're sick of doing. Quote follow-ups, call notes into a brief, the reply you keep putting off.
  2. Run it in Chat with a proper brief. Who you are, who it's for, how you sound, what good looks like.
  3. The third time you retype that brief, save it. A Project in Chat, or a folder in Cowork. That's your first context file.
  4. Curious about Code? One folder, one CLAUDE.md from the starter template in the guide, the same one job.

Post what you made in the group, or bring it to the next call.
