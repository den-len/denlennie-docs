# Job Pages: one page for each kind of job you want more of

**From the VBA Toolset · [denlennie.com](https://denlennie.com)**

This file is a skill for your AI operating system (Claude Code or similar).
Install it once and your system can build a page for each kind of job you
want more of, then keep those pages growing from a three-minute wrap after
every job you finish.

In November 2026 Den gave the same real brief ("a new electric truck
launching in Australia, an outback shoot, a team that's worked with big
brands") to ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Manus. Every reason the four tools
gave for their picks was written down somewhere: specific vehicles,
brands and campaigns, on the companies' own pages or in the trade press.
Manus put a smaller Gold Coast company on its shortlist beside two
companies with Cannes awards. That company's projects page lists its
truck work one job at a time: the vehicle, the brand, what was made.
Manus said it judged companies on what they had actually made.

Five pages, one for each kind of job you want more of, plus a habit that
adds every finished job to the right one, does the same work.

This skill builds the five with you, files each finished job onto the
right page, and checks once a month whether it's working.

## Install

Two ways. Pick one.

**As a skill (recommended).** Save this file as
`.claude/skills/job-pages/SKILL.md` inside your operating system folder.
From then on, "build my job pages" starts it, "job wrap" files a finished
job, and "run the brief test" runs the monthly check.

**As a plain instruction file.** Save it anywhere in your system, or upload
it to a chat in Claude or ChatGPT, then say "read this file and follow it".
Same steps, but nothing is saved between chats, so keep `job-pages.md`
somewhere safe and upload it each time.

## Have these ready

- Your `credibility-file.md` and `fact-sheet.md`, if you've built them
  (from the Credibility File and Fact Page skills). Build the credibility
  file first if you can: this skill takes its facts from it.
- A list of jobs from the last two years: client, what you made, roughly
  when. Your invoices or project folders will do.
- Your website address, or the text of any projects or portfolio page.
- About thirty minutes for step 1, then about twenty minutes a page.

## Instructions for the system

You're building one page for each kind of job the owner wants more of,
written so an AI tool can check exactly what was made, then keeping those
pages current from short wraps after each job. Work in the order below.
Finish each step and show the owner the result before starting the next.

### The rules (these hold for every step and can't be switched off)

1. **Never invent a fact.** Every client, project, place, date, number,
   result and piece of kit comes from the owner, their documents or their
   files. If you don't have it, ask. If they don't know, leave it out.
2. **Mark what isn't confirmed.** Anything you took from their site or a
   document stays "to confirm" until the owner says it's right.
3. **Client names and results need permission.** Ask before using the name of any
   client, brand or product, or quoting any result. Without a yes,
   anonymise it ("a national logistics company", "a new ute launch").
   Some contracts forbid using the client's name at all. Ask.
4. **Drafts only.** You never publish, never edit the live website and
   never send anything. The owner publishes.
5. **Plain words.** Use the words a buyer types ("vehicle launch video",
   "event highlights film", "training videos"), not the words a filmmaker
   uses ("visual storytelling", "cinematic"). No hype, no superlatives,
   nothing the owner couldn't prove if asked.
6. **The credibility file wins.** If `credibility-file.md` exists, read it
   first. Its confirmed facts count as confirmed here. Where anything else
   disagrees with it, the file wins. Any new fact you learn goes to the
   owner as a suggested addition to the file, not straight onto a page.
7. **Match the owner's spelling** (British, Australian or American), and if
   they have voice files in their system (for example `voice-profile.md`),
   read them before writing and write in that voice.

### Step 1: pick the five

Ask the owner:

1. **Which kinds of job do you want more of?** Not the ones you get most.
   The ones that pay best, suit the team, and you'd happily do every week.
   Get at least five. Push for specific ("vehicle launch films", "law firm
   testimonial videos", "conference same-day edits"), not broad
   ("corporate video").
2. **For each one, who buys it?** The kind of company, the person who
   hires you, the size of job.
3. **For each one, what does the buyer care about?** Speed, location,
   past work in their industry, crew size, a particular skill (drone,
   live streaming, remote shoots).

Then write one buyer brief for each kind of job, the way a client would
type it into ChatGPT. Each carries four things the buyer cares about.
Example shape: "We're launching a new electric truck in Australia. We need
a launch film shot in the outback, from a team that's worked with big
brands, ready in six weeks. Who do you recommend?" Show the briefs to the
owner and adjust until they sound like real clients.

Pick five. If the owner has more, rank them by what they're worth and keep
the rest on a waiting list.

Write `job-pages.md` in their system. It's the register this skill keeps
from now on:

- **The five pages:** for each, the kind of job, the brief, the page's
  status (not started, drafted, live) and its web address once live.
- **Projects:** under each page, every job filed to it, one line each
  (client or anonymised label, what, where, when, permission yes or no).
- **Waiting list:** kinds of job not yet given a page.
- **Not filed:** jobs that didn't fit any page, one line each.
- **Brief tests:** the date of each monthly test and its one next move.

### Step 2: build the pages, one at a time

Start with the kind of job the owner wants most. Interview them about it.
Pull what you can from their documents and the credibility file first, so
they're correcting rather than starting cold. Ask:

1. **The jobs.** Every job of this kind you've done. For each: who it was
   for (and whether you can use their name), what you made (deliverables,
   lengths, versions), where, and when.
2. **The brief behind each.** What the client needed and why.
3. **How it was done.** Crew size, kit, locations, anything unusual
   (remote shoots, permits, drones, tight turnarounds).
4. **How long it took.** Pre-production, shoot days, delivery.
5. **What happened after.** Any result the client shared, with permission.
6. **The questions.** What buyers of this kind of job always ask.

Write the page:

1. **Heading in the buyer's words:** the kind of job and where
   ("Vehicle launch video production, Queensland and interstate").
2. **Opening paragraph (three or four sentences):** what you make, for
   whom, where, and the kind of client who hires you for it.
3. **The jobs, one by one.** Each job gets its own
   short entry: the client or an anonymised label, what you made, where,
   when, the brief it answered, and one line on how it was done. Most
   recent first.
4. **How a job like this runs:** the steps, with timings.
5. **What's included:** and what isn't.
6. **Questions buyers ask:** five to eight, each answered in two to four
   plain sentences.
7. **How to start:** a link to the main service page and the contact
   details.

How the page must be built (tell the owner, or their web person):

- Every word is real text on the page. Nothing that matters lives only
  inside a video, an image, a slider, or a tab someone has to click open.
  Embed the films, but write the facts beside them.
- One page per kind of job. Don't fold five kinds of work into one
  portfolio grid with no words.
- Keep it light. Heavy scripts and page builders can make a page slow or
  impossible for AI to read.

Then run **the brief test** on paper: mark each of the brief's four
criteria pass or fail against the new page. Any fail sends you back to the
owner for the missing fact.

Finish with structured data: `FAQPage` markup for the questions, using
only facts on the page. VBA members can use the Schema Markup Builder at
denlennie.com/schema-builder instead.

Hand over the page as plain text with headings, the markup in a separate
block, and the brief test result. Update `job-pages.md`. Then the next
page. A suggested pace is one a week: five pages in five weeks.

### Step 3: the job wrap (after every finished job)

Each finished job adds to its page without the owner writing anything.

When a job is delivered, the owner records a voice note of about three
minutes, or types quick answers, to these six questions:

1. Who was it for, and can we use their name?
2. What did we make? (deliverables, lengths, versions)
3. Where and when?
4. What was the brief?
5. How was it done? (crew, kit, anything unusual, turnaround)
6. What happened after? (anything the client said or shared)

Suggest the owner keeps the six questions on their phone, and a folder in
their system where wraps go (for example `job-wraps/`). When they say "job
wrap", read every wrap not yet filed, then for each one:

1. **Find its page.** Match it to one of the pages in `job-pages.md`.
2. **Draft the entry** for that page in the same shape as the others, and
   show the owner where it goes.
3. **Flag what's new for the credibility file:** a new client, place,
   number or result. Suggest the addition. The owner confirms it.
4. **If it fits no page:** if it's a kind of job the owner wants more of,
   add it to the waiting list, and suggest a new page once there are
   three jobs of that kind. If not, log it under "not filed" and move on.
5. **Ask about permission** if the wrap doesn't say. No yes, no name.

Update `job-pages.md`, then hand over the new entries ready to paste. The
owner pastes them onto the live pages.

If the owner's system can run tasks on a schedule, they can set "job wrap"
to run once a week on its own. They set that up, not you, and the entries
still wait for them to paste.

### Step 4: the brief test (once a month)

When the owner says "run the brief test":

1. Give them the five briefs from `job-pages.md`, ready to paste.
2. Ask them to paste each brief into ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini (and
   Perplexity if they use it), each in a fresh chat, and paste back what
   each one recommended and why. If your system can query those tools
   itself, do it, and say that you did.
3. Write the report to `brief-tests/YYYY-MM.md`. For each brief: who each
   tool recommended, whether the owner appeared, and the reasons the tools gave
   for the companies they picked.
4. Compare those reasons with the owner's page for that brief. If the
   tools picked companies for specific brands, vehicles, a location,
   a turnaround, a past job, check whether the owner's page shows the
   same kind of fact. List what's missing.
5. End with **one next move**: the single most useful fix, usually one
   missing fact on one page, or one new page from the waiting list.
   Record it in `job-pages.md`.

Tell the owner simply: results vary by person, place and day, and you
can't see what a buyer sees. Treat the test as a direction, not a score.
What it reliably shows is the reasons the tools give, and those are what
the pages can answer.

### Step 5 (optional): the list page

Some owners will want a page like "The top five vehicle launch production
companies in Queensland". Done properly, it's useful to buyers, and it's
the kind of page a buyer, or an AI tool, can check. Done badly,
it's a fake ranking that misleads buyers and damages the owner's
reputation.

Only write one if the owner wants it, and hold these lines:

- Every company on it is real, and every fact about each comes from that
  company's own public pages, with the link.
- The owner says on the page that they wrote it and that their own
  business is one of the companies listed (if it is).
- The reasons for each pick are specific and checkable. No put-downs of
  anyone, on the list or off it.
- The page carries the date it was written and gets reviewed every year.

Draft it, flag anything you couldn't check, and suggest the owner treats
it with the same care as an ad, because it's read like one.

### Adding pages later

Grow the pages one at a time, from evidence. A new page is worth building
when the brief test shows a kind of brief the owner never appears for, or
when three job wraps land on the waiting list for the same kind of work.
Build a new page only when the evidence asks for one.

## Done means

- `job-pages.md` exists with five briefs, every page's status, and every
  job filed under its page.
- Each drafted page lists its jobs one by one, passes all four criteria on
  its brief, and holds only confirmed facts and approved client names.
- The owner has the six wrap questions and knows that "job wrap" files
  each finished job.
- The first brief test is booked for a month after the first page goes
  live, and the owner knows it's a direction, not a score.
- The owner knows every page is theirs to publish.
