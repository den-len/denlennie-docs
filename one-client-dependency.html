When one big client owns your video business

A big anchor client is a gift and a risk at the same time. While it lasts it pays for everything, and in the back of your mind you're thinking: I hope it never ends, I know it probably will. Don't wait for the day it goes. Work out who your most profitable clients are, then go and find more clients like them.

Why it happens

I've lived this. For twelve or thirteen years I was Sony's go-to producer for their camera launch films in the UK. A massive client, constantly feeding me work. I always knew it would end one day, and eventually it did. It was a great gravy train while it lasted. The trouble is that when one client keeps you that busy, you never have the time to build the alternative. You're too busy dealing with what's in front of you.

There's also a hidden cost owners miss. You scale up around that client to serve them, which means staff and freelancers you wouldn't need without them. If they leave, you lose the revenue and you're left carrying the team.

The way out isn't to push the big client away. It's to stop relying on luck and referrals and build a marketing engine you can switch on. The question to ask yourself is simple: if they disappeared tomorrow, what would you do on Monday morning?

Try this

Audit every client: total revenue, number of jobs, average spend, time spent and margin, worked down to margin per day. The top of that list tells you who to go and find more of, so your biggest client stays a choice rather than a dependency.
