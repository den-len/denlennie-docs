Your morning briefing, in Cowork.

The setup is the teaching moment. Show them the whole thing is a one-minute click flow, then it just works every morning after. Not a build, a login.

01 · Connect once

Three connectors, about a minute

  1. Open Cowork and click Customize in the left sidebar, then Connectors. The screen splits into web connectors and desktop connectors. The ones you want, Gmail, Google Calendar and Slack, are web connectors.
  2. Click the + icon, then Browse Connectors. Find Gmail near the top, click the + next to it. A Google sign-in window opens: pick your account, review the permissions, click Continue. It drops back into Cowork with Gmail showing as connected. Under a minute.
  3. Repeat the same flow for Google Calendar, then Slack. Same three moves each time: plus icon, pick the connector, authorise. The repetition is good for the room, they watch Gmail once and they've understood the whole pattern.

Say this out loud while you click (it's what builds trust)

  • It only sees what you see. Claude mirrors your own login and permissions. It can't reach anything you can't reach yourself.
  • Nothing changes without you. By default, write and delete actions need your approval. Calendar edits, sending email, none of it happens silently. Claude shows you what it's about to do first.
One constraint, know it before you stand up. The Google connector authenticates one Google account at a time, and Gmail, Calendar and Drive all ride on that single account. Decide which account is "the business day" before you start, connect that one, and don't mention the other. If someone asks: multi-account isn't supported yet, and the common workaround is forwarding from the second account into the connected one.

02 · Ask for your day

The prompt

Once the three are connected, paste this in, or just say it. The first run is the demo; from tomorrow it's a five-second habit.

The prompt 
Give me my briefing for the day ahead.

From my calendar today: a quick summary first (how many meetings, my first and last, any big gaps), then each meeting in order with the time, who's on it, and a one-line note on what it's about or what I should prepare.

From my email: anything that needs a reply today, and anything tied to one of today's meetings.

Then a short, prioritised task list: the three or four things that actually matter today, in order.

Keep it tight and scannable. Don't change anything, just tell me what's on.

After the first run, "What's on today?" is enough. That's the point: you taught it the shape once, now it just answers.

The payoff line

Not a build. A login.

Connect three things once, and from tomorrow you just open Cowork and ask for your day. That's the operating system, and it took you a minute to switch on.
