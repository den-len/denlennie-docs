How a video business owner stopped guessing on every quote

Anonymised from a real coaching situation. No names or identifying details.

The situation

An owner who had quoted a hundred jobs still seized up on every one. He'd work out a number, get his finger over send, and stop: "does that sound fair?" It wasn't really a question about the number. It was a question about the method, and the method was missing.

What was really going on

With no system, every quote was a fresh act of nerve. He'd feel for a figure, second-guess it, and because fear was doing the pricing, he'd land low. Then he'd send it and brace for the pushback. He'd done this so many times he assumed the anxiety was just part of the job. It wasn't a confidence flaw. It was a missing tool.

What changed

He stopped trying to find a price and started building one, the same way every time, from four parts he could name: his time (all of it, not just the camera time), his gear, his overhead, and his margin as a line rather than a leftover. Add those up and he had a floor he could stand on. The number was no longer a feeling he was defending. It was a calculation he could walk a client through.

The result

The agony went away the moment the guessing did. When a client pushed, he wasn't improvising any more, he was explaining, and a price you can explain is a price you can hold. The relief, more than the revenue, was the thing he noticed first.
