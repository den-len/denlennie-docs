01Where the business actually is

Both diagnostics, run separately, land in the same place.

Elite positioning sitting on a business with no engine underneath it.

Carl runs the exact business most VBA members are trying to become, and he has arrived from the opposite direction. The niche is dialled (university researchers and the comms professionals who commission for them). The method exists and is written down (the journalist's W questions, then a built-for-camera metaphor). The proof is the kind most video businesses never touch: a 50-episode Cambridge science TV show, a live BBC relationship, a Feedspot top-five podcast, University of Cambridge departments and colleges on the client list.

And almost none of it is wired to anything. The lead magnets are switched off. The site has had no analytics for over a year. Enquiries leak at the first reply. The pipeline is passive and referral-fed. There is no pricing frame, no follow-up cadence, no packaged offer to say yes to.

The one-line version

This is an activation and conversion job, not a build job.

That is rare, and it should shape the whole engagement. Nothing upstream needs reinventing. The work is switching on what exists and giving it a frame to convert through.

02The opportunity, in three layers

Layer one

The leak

Enquiries arrive and die at Carl's first reply, which offers scope options over email. That hands over free thinking and removes the reason to get on a call, which is where Carl wins (his own words: prospects convert when he demonstrates understanding and sketches ideas off the cuff). Two finished lead magnets sit deactivated. Nothing is measured. Fixing this costs almost no creative energy and stops value draining today.

Layer two

The frame

There is no packaged offer, so every enquiry reopens the menu and the buyer sets the price. The fix is one named partnership (working name: the Research Impact Partnership), a defined asset suite from a single shoot (hero documentary, funding and REF cut, public engagement cutdowns, distribution advice), an anchor price, a floor, and the Intellectual Integrity Guarantee against the one fear he sells against. Drafted and ready to react to.

Layer three

The gold

The Diagnostic Master and Carl's own account point at the same mechanism from two directions. The Master's insight: the sub-£5K assumption is a timing gap, not a budget ceiling, because research films get funded specifically, at £5K to £20K, through a grant application. Carl's stated major constraint, in his own words: research fellows never quite know how much money they have, because budgets are applied for; around £10K is usually available without much trouble. But his next sentence gives the game away: once they have seen what he can do, they generally want more, and those things grow over time.

That is not a constraint. That is a land-and-expand revenue model running by accident. The budget is not fixed, it is written. The fellow with £10K today is the fellow with £20K next round if the film is written into the funding application, and Carl already owns the asset that starts that conversation (the budget framing in his own Creating a Video Brief guide). It is just never deployed early enough to reframe the number before the number kills the deal. Making this deliberate is likely the highest-margin single change available.

04Discussion points

The places where the two diagnostics differ, or where the call is Carl's and Den's to make. These are the agenda for the first session.

1

Leak first or package first?

The Diagnostic Master says plug the conversion leak before anything else. The OS diagnostic says package the offer first, because a fixed first reply still needs something to point at. Recommendation: both inside the first month. They are not in conflict. The leak-plugging is mechanical (weeks 1 to 2); the packaging is strategic (weeks 3 to 4); the rewritten reply gets sharper the moment the package exists.

2

Is the offer actually done?

The Master treats positioning and offer as complete and warns against redoing them. The OS read splits them: positioning done, offer packaging not (a menu with no anchor, floor or guarantee). The test is simple: put the Research Impact Partnership one-pager in front of Carl. If it resonates, the packaging was the missing piece. If he pushes back, that is the conversation.

3

Where does the anchor sit? On a ladder that matches the funding cycle.

The Offer Architect argued £25K to £50K anchored against grant and REF value. The funded reality for an individual fellow is roughly £10K without a special application. These stop conflicting the moment the offer is shaped like the funding system itself:

  • Entry  A first film sized deliberately (not apologetically) to the no-application budget, around £10K. This is the easy yes.
  • Expansion  The next project written into the following grant round, £15K to £25K plus, using the funding-timing reframe. This is where "they generally want more" becomes a designed step instead of a happy accident.
  • Institutional  The department, college or REF 2029 campaign layer, where the £25K to £50K anchor genuinely lives, because that buyer is an institution with impact budget, not a fellow with a discretionary pot.

The numbers get set with the audit on the table. The rule that matters now: no entry film ships without the expansion path named in the proposal.

What REF is. The Research Excellence Framework is the UK-wide assessment of university research quality, run by the four higher education funding bodies roughly every seven years. Its results steer around £2 billion a year of public research funding, which is why universities take it seriously. The next cycle is REF 2029.

A quarter of the assessment weight sits on engagement and impact, evidenced through impact case studies: documented accounts of how a piece of research changed something outside academia. Documentary film is exactly the kind of evidence those case studies use, and the people who assemble them (research offices, impact officers, principal investigators) are the institutional buyers this rung of the ladder points at.

Fellows do not deal with REF day to day, which is why the term may be unfamiliar even inside the niche. That is the tell, not a gap: the buyers one level up live inside REF machinery, and the budgets attached to it are planned, not rustled up. To validate with Carl's own university contacts before the institutional layer is built on it.

4

One avatar at the front, or two?

The StoryBrand work gives the Researcher and the Comms Lead equal billing. The money, the respect and the referrals sit with the researcher and the PI who controls funding. The comms lead is also where the sub-£5K drain and sign-off politics live. Recommendation: researcher front door, comms lead served as a secondary lane. Confirm Carl agrees before the homepage rewrite bakes it in.

5

Constraint or story?

Carl presents the funding uncertainty as his major constraint, and we take him at his word as the starting point. Structurally it is real: fellows do not control large budgets on demand. But his own evidence (clients want more once they have seen the work, and it grows over time) says the £10K ceiling is an entry point, not a limit. Before accepting the constraint at face value, two other routes are worth testing openly with him:

  • Sell one level up. The PI, the head of department, the research office, the college development office. That is where REF and public-engagement budgets sit, and the £10K lumpiness largely disappears. The REF angle came from the Offer Architect's market analysis, not from Carl, and that is the point: the buyers one level up live inside REF machinery he has not needed to touch yet. Worth validating with his own university contacts before building on it.
  • The series model Carl himself floated. His intake suggests making "a TV show with a presenter where the researcher is the guest, not the host". A department funding an ongoing series is the natural shape of an institutional retainer: it smooths the funding lumpiness for both sides and turns per-project uncertainty into scheduled output. Elemental Ideas, his 50-episode channel, is the proof he can already run this format.

Neither replaces the researcher entry point. They sit above it, and they are the honest test of whether the constraint is the market's or the meaning is Carl's.

The short version

The business is better than its systems.

Nothing about the positioning, the method or the proof needs rebuilding, which makes this the rarest kind of engagement: switch on what exists, package it so buyers can say yes at the right number, and shape the offer like the funding system his clients actually live in, an easy £10K yes that is designed to grow into the next grant round. What Carl calls his major constraint is, on his own evidence, his growth model waiting to be run on purpose. The quarter is won or lost in the first four weeks: plug the leak, package the offer, and everything after that compounds.
