04

What the thread is actually telling you

The real signal

The rushes question is never about the files.

The clients who ask for rushes are the ones who were not fully sold on what they commissioned. Either the scope was vague, the value was not established, or the brief drifted and they are hedging by asking for the raw material.

A client who believes in the finished film does not ask for the footage any more than a satisfied restaurant customer asks for the ingredients.

When a client asks for rushes, the right question is not "what do I owe them legally?" It is: "where did I lose them?"

This matters specifically for where Cambridge TV is heading. The Research Impact Documentary Partnership positioning — everything from the Offer Architect work at the London workshop — is built on a fundamentally different client relationship. Partners do not ask for the recipe. They hired you for the result.

When clients understand they are commissioning a research impact asset against a REF 2029 deliverable, the rushes question rarely comes up. The brief is different. The relationship is different. The question stops being asked.

The positioning shift that makes this obsolete. When a client commissions a Research Impact Documentary Partnership — not "a film" — they are buying the outcome. That is not a T&Cs question. It is a category question. You are in the process of building the category.
